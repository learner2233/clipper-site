The privacy policy in six parts

What Clipper receives

When you connect an account, Clipper receives an access token from that platform and your account's display name. For TikTok it uses the user.info.basic scope (your display name) and the video.upload scope (to send approved clips to your drafts).

Where Clipper keeps it

Tokens, clips and settings are stored only on the computer where Clipper runs, in a file only its owner can read. Clipper has no servers of its own, collects no analytics, and does not sell your data or share it beyond what this policy lists.

How Clipper uses it

Your tokens are used only to send the clips you approve to your own accounts, to show which account is connected, and, about once an hour, to read the views, likes and comments of clips Clipper posted there in the last 30 days. This covers YouTube, Instagram and Facebook; on Instagram, Clipper finds its clips among the account's recent posts. TikTok drafts are not read. The counts are kept with the clips.

What Clipper sends to the AI service

The AI service its owner has chosen receives the spoken words of a video, a summary of when its chat, sound and replays peak, and four small frames of each clip Clipper is about to keep, to tell gameplay from cutscenes. Translated versions send a clip's captions, title and description. When ranking new clips, Clipper also sends the titles, opening lines and view counts of its five most-viewed posted clips. Tokens and the names of connected accounts are never sent.

Removing your data from Clipper

Disconnect the account in Clipper to delete its token, or remove Clipper's access in the platform's own settings (for TikTok: Settings and privacy, Security and permissions, Apps and services permissions).

Changes to this privacy policy

This policy may change; the date above shows the latest version.
